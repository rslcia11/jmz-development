# Copy — voice and rules

How JMZ writes on the site. Every new sentence should pass these checks.

## Positioning in one line

JMZ builds custom software — management systems and ERP, AI and websites — that
puts things in order and helps the client grow. The visitor brings a problem or
an idea; JMZ brings the system.

Audience is global: companies, institutions, entrepreneurs and individuals.
Never write "tu negocio" as if every client were a business; use "tú", "cómo
trabajas", "clientes", "organización".

## Principles (and why)

1. **Clarity over cleverness.** A visitor must know what JMZ builds within
   five seconds. B2B visitors who understand the offer that fast convert 2–3×
   more; clever or abstract headlines lose.
   ([Apexure](https://www.apexure.com/blog/landing-page-conversion-rate-optimisation-tips-clarity-is-everything/),
   [Heyflow](https://heyflow.com/blog/b2b-landing-page-best-practices/))
2. **Objective, not promotional.** No marketese. In NN/g's study, objective
   copy measured 27% better usability; concise +58%; concise + scannable +
   objective +124%.
   ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/))
3. **Specific beats generic.** Name deliverables (in body copy: ERP, AI models, websites)
   and concrete behaviours, not adjectives. Generic positioning is why most dev
   agency sites read the same.
   ([100signals](https://100signals.com/best-positioning-agencies-for-software-development-companies/),
   [April Dunford](https://businessofsoftware.org/talks/speakers/april-dunford/))
4. **Headlines encompass; subheads land.** A headline never lists
   deliverables (a list doesn't scale: 20 projects can't fit in a title and it
   reads like a catalogue). It states a capability in JMZ's own words — "Convertimos
   tu idea en software.", "Sistemas que ordenan tu operación" —
   and the subhead or paragraph right below carries the concrete "what". That
   keeps the five-second clarity test without turning titles into inventories.
   The H1 still names the category people search for ("software a medida"), so
   a first-time visitor and Google both know what is sold.
5. **Two doors in: problem and opportunity.** Not every visitor is stuck; some
   want to improve or launch something new. Copy speaks to both ("resolver o
   crear", "el problema o la idea") instead of framing every client as someone
   with a pain. Warm, second person, no fear tactics.
6. **Lower the risk next to every ask.** A CTA can carry a short reassurance
   ("Si no somos la opción correcta, te lo diremos") so the click feels safe.
   Only promise what the owner has explicitly confirmed — never add offers
   (free consultation, response times, guarantees) on your own.
7. **Commitments instead of claims.** "Tiempos y alcance, siempre claros"
   plus what it means in practice says more than "somos transparentes".
8. **True or out.** No invented clients, metrics, testimonials or awards
   (master plan §30, §76). Every entry in `src/data/projects.ts` must stay
   traceable to a real project (owner confirmation or public repo).

## Voice

- Spanish, neutral Latin American, *tuteo* ("tu negocio", "te lo diremos").
- First person plural for JMZ ("construimos", "entendemos").
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Technical mono labels (eyebrows, categories) stay in English, as a design
  convention; headings and body are Spanish.

## Banned phrases

"soluciones innovadoras", "transformamos tu negocio", "llevamos tu empresa al
siguiente nivel", "de vanguardia", "líderes en", "pasión por", "360°",
"sinergia", "tecnología de punta", "a la medida de tus sueños" — and any
sentence that would fit any other agency's site unchanged.

## Headline test

Before shipping a heading, ask: could a competitor publish this exact sentence?
If yes, rewrite it until only JMZ could say it truthfully.

Then **verify it**: search the exact phrase (and close variants) on the web.
Found on 2026-09-27 and therefore rejected: "Si lo puedes explicar/imaginar…"
(common saying + trademark), "Cuéntanos tu idea" (IQ Latam), "Antes de
escribir código, entendemos tu negocio" (LunaLABS), "Hablas con quien
construye" (Koder Crafters), "Que nada se te escape" (Ingenia Bit).

Also rejected by the owner: "Tú pones el para qué" (a riddle, not clear) and
"Tu idea todavía no es software. Por ahora.".

## Hero headline

"Convertimos tu idea en software." — the owner's choice, kept as is. The
subhead opens the problem door ("Y también ese problema que te quita tiempo").
